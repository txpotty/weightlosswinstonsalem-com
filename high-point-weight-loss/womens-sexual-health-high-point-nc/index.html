    Understanding Low Desire in High Point

    Low sexual desire is one of the most common sexual health concerns women report. When it is persistent, causes distress and is not explained by another condition or medication, it may be diagnosed as hypoactive sexual desire disorder, or HSDD. More than one factor is common. Antidepressants, hormonal birth control, perimenopause, thyroid problems, pain, poor sleep and relationship strain can all contribute. A good evaluation looks at each.

    Low Libido Treatment for Women

    Treatment starts with finding what changed. Your provider reviews medications known to lower desire, asks about menopause symptoms, thyroid history and pain with sex, and looks at sleep and stress. Sometimes the answer is small. Addressing dryness or changing an antidepressant with your prescriber sometimes helps more than a new prescription. Medication options follow if needed.

    Flibanserin for Women in High Point

    Flibanserin, sold as Addyi, is FDA-approved for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. The dose is one 100 mg tablet each night at bedtime, and it works on serotonin and dopamine rather than hormones. Results build slowly. Providers usually reassess at eight weeks and stop it if there is no benefit. Alcohol timing matters with flibanserin.

    PT-141 for Women

    PT-141, or bremelanotide, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. It is a 1.75 mg injection under the skin of the belly or thigh, given at least 45 minutes before sex, and it acts on melanocortin receptors in the brain linked to desire. Nausea is common at first. The limits are one dose per day and eight per month. Compounded versions are not FDA-approved.

    Oxytocin Therapy in High Point

    Oxytocin is a hormone involved in bonding, touch and arousal. Compounded oxytocin is sometimes prescribed as a nasal spray or troche taken before intimacy to support arousal and closeness. It is an off-label use. There is no FDA approval for this use, the evidence is thin and responses differ a lot from woman to woman. Your provider explains the evidence honestly.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Menopausal status comes first. Both FDA-approved drugs are labeled for premenopausal women, which means postmenopausal women often get a different plan. Daily or as-needed is next. Regular drinkers often lean toward PT-141, women with blood pressure issues toward flibanserin, and liver function shapes the choice too. Your preferences count as well.

    Side Effects and Safety in High Point

    Flibanserin can cause dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and low blood pressure. Alcohol and CYP3A4-blocking drugs make those effects worse, which explains the bedtime dose and drinking rules. Nausea is common with PT-141. It also causes flushing and headache, briefly raises blood pressure and can darken skin with repeated use. Oxytocin can cause headache or nasal irritation.
